Every time you *upgrade* your iPhone, you throw away real gold.
Real *gold*. Inside your phone. Inside every phone.
This is what *recycling* plants do, so please, don't try this at home.
It only takes three *simple* steps.
First, the *battery* comes out.
This part is very, very *important*.
If the battery gets crushed, or goes in the liquid, it can catch *fire*.
Now look *closer*.
These tiny shiny bits on the charging port? That's *gold*.
These little pins? Gold. And inside some chips, there's gold wires thinner than a *hair*.
Next, the board goes into a special *liquid*.
Slowly, slowly, the clear liquid turns *blue*.
That's the *copper* leaving, and the board falls apart into mud.
Black, muddy, *ugly* mud.
But once the bubbling stops, look. The mud starts to *sparkle*.
That sparkle? That's the *gold*.
Wash it, *rinse* it, wash it again.
Then a stronger acid melts the gold right into the liquid, and it turns *yellow*.
One more powder goes in, and once the bubbling stops again, a brown dust falls to the *bottom*.
This brown dust? This is pure *gold*.
Gold sand, gold dust, whatever you call it, it's *gold*.
Now heat it. Heat it more. Heat it until it *glows*.
Pour it, and *there* it is.
A real gold *bar*.
Now the honest part.
One phone gives you about three *pounds* of gold.
Three pounds.
So for this bar, you just need about *thirty thousand* more phones.
Still, a pile of old phones holds way more gold than a pile of gold *mine* rock.
So how many old phones are sitting in your *drawer* right now?
Before you sell or recycle one, *erase* it first, because your photos and bank apps are still inside.
