# Phoneverse: "Cheers, Biscuit" (draft 2)

**Premise:** You start explaining that thieves really want your password, not your phone, and two blokes nick your phone mid-sentence. Three months later a "your phone's been found" text arrives, and Jess finishes the job for them.

**Real safety point:** after a theft, scammers send fake "your lost phone has been found" texts. The link is a fake login. If they get your Apple Account password, they can take Activation Lock off and sell the phone whole.

**Changes from draft 1 (your notes):**
1. New hook: a paradox stated as fact, not a question.
2. Casual British tone throughout: short lines, slang, people talking over each other.
3. Two thieves, planted in the background before the snatch, a real struggle, then a split in two directions.
4. No dead space: every frame has a voice, a sound effect or music. The silent beats are gone.
5. A lip-sync plan for every line: lips ON only where they help, as in the reference.

Target **66 s**. 229 spoken words at about 3.5 words/s, with the gaps filled by sound effects.

## Directing: the snatch scene (0:00-0:20)
This scene makes or breaks the hook, so here it is shot by shot.

**Foreshadowing: viewers see it, You don't**
| Time | Plant | How |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | Thief A sits on the **yellow e-bike** at the kerb behind her, not moving, watching | Background, in focus but not centred. A rewatcher will catch it. |
| 0:03 ("Watch.") | The focus **shifts off her for half a second** to the bus stop, where Thief B pockets his own phone and gives A a tiny nod. A nods back | She keeps talking. "Watch" now means two things. |
| 0:05 | Thief B steps out of the crowd **into her path**, walking towards her | Camera walking backwards in front of her, so he grows in frame behind her shoulder |

**The struggle (0:07.5-0:12)**
1. **Bump (0:07.5):** B clips her shoulder; she turns half left. His "Oh, sorry, love!" pulls her attention away from the kerb.
2. **Grab (0:08.5):** A rolls in from the right on the e-bike and grabs the phone. She **clamps it with both hands**. A 1.5 s tug-of-war: the bike wobbles, she's dragged a step, shoppers gasp.
3. **Free (0:10.5):** A yanks it loose. Speed ramp: the phone **spins through the air** in slow motion for 6 frames, then snaps back to full speed as **B catches it**.
4. **The wipe (0:11.5):** a red double-decker bus passes between the camera and them for half a second.
5. **The split (0:12):** the bus clears. A is pedalling **right**, B is running **left** into the crowd, both clutching something to their chest. Nobody, viewers included, can tell who has it.
6. **The spin (0:12-0:15):** whip pans left and right with her head on "which one's got it?!", then the two bystanders point in opposite directions.
7. **The stop (0:15):** she takes two steps and stops. "Nah. I'm not chasing two grown men." This keeps our safety message (never chase) and gets a laugh.
8. **The camera leaves her (0:18):** it whips left and chases **B**: he has it. She shrinks in the background, waving and shouting.

**Safety rules for this scene:** it shows the outcome (bump, grab, split) as drama with no technical detail, so it stays within the character sheet's "outcome, never a how-to" rule. It also warns people that thieves often work in pairs. Neither thief is shown as worse than the other: both take part, and the camera picks one only because it has to follow the phone.

## No dead space: the rules for the edit
- No gap longer than **0.3 s** without a voice. Any gap is filled by a sound effect: whoosh, bike, bus horn, gasps, text ping, box thud.
- People talk over each other in the struggle and with the bystanders. Lines overlap by 0.1-0.3 s, like real people.
- Transitions carry voice: the montage and the time jump play under voice-over, never under silence.
- The music never fully drops out. It dips under the dialogue and swells on the transitions.
- Street noise sits under the whole of scene 1, so the struggle feels busy.

## Lip-sync plan
- **ON (synced)** for 15 of the 28 lines: each character's first line, every punchline, every face-to-face moment. These are the lines where seeing the mouth sells the emotion.
- **OFF** for voice-over, montage, wide shots, the camera leaving her, and the hooded figure (his face is never shown, which also keeps the mystery).
- In the reference video, lips show only on short, emotional lines. The same here: no lips-ON shot runs longer than about 3 s.
- Make each character's voice as its own audio file, then drive the mouth shapes from that file.
