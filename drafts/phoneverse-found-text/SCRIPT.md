# Phoneverse: "Cheers, Biscuit" (draft 3)

**Premise:** You ask why thieves keep nicking phones they don't even want, then two blokes nick yours mid-sentence. Three months later a "your phone's been found" text arrives, and Jess finishes the job for them.

**Real safety point:** after a theft, scammers send fake "your lost phone has been found" texts. The link is a fake login. With your Apple Account password they can take Activation Lock off and sell the phone whole.

**Changes from draft 2 (your notes):**
1. Your hook, with a hook inside the hook: an open question the video never answers out loud. The last line answers it with a picture.
2. The swear word is bleeped.
3. Thief B is now huge.
4. Emotion direction for every line, tuned for Kokoro.
5. Sound effects always sit under the voices.
6. Original background music for every scene.

**Rendered length: 79 s** ("JESS!" at 1:17.4, then its echo). All audio is generated by `audio/make_audio.py`; the music is in `audio/music_garage.py`.

**Render check (26 Nov):**
- All 31 Kokoro lines were transcribed back with Whisper, and every one reads as the script.
- Mix: -14.0 LUFS, true peak -1.2 dBTP.
- Scene times: hook 0:00-0:10, snatch 0:10-0:27, journey 0:27-0:43, sofa 0:43-1:15, warehouse 1:15-1:19.
- Lines fixed after the first check:
  - L05: "nah" needed its sound spelled out (it was heard as "now").
  - L07: slowed down (it was heard as "the go").
  - L14: "Oi, camera!" (plain "Oi!" was heard as "Or").
  - L23: "Oh, come on" (it was heard as "oh four").
  - L31: the echo now starts later, so "JESS!" isn't heard as "yes".

## The hook
| | Line |
|---|---|
| Your draft | "If UK thieves don't really want phone, why the fuck do they want it. Sounds mad right? Now judge this, please. I'm walking down..." |
| **Final** | **"If UK thieves don't really want your phone... why the [BLEEP] do they keep nicking it? Sounds mad, right? Now you judge this."** |

Why it works:
- **An open question in 2 seconds** that nobody can answer. The video never says the answer. "Cheers, Biscuit" shows it: they wanted the password. Viewers who miss that will ask in the comments, which is free engagement.
- **The bleep breaks the pattern.** A sound that cuts off the voice in second 2, while the mouth keeps moving, makes people rewatch to lip-read it.
- "keep nicking it" is casual, and "keep" hints at how often it happens (close to 200 phones a day in London).
- "Now you judge this" hands the verdict to the viewer, so they watch to the end to decide.

**Bleep rules:**
- The audio is a 1 kHz bleep at voice level, replacing the word exactly. `make_audio.py` does this automatically from Kokoro's word timings.
- Captions read "why the 🤬". Never type the word in captions, the description or on-screen text: TikTok moderates text more strictly than bleeped audio.
- One small risk: Mum is our share target, and a bleeped swear is mild. Keep it at one swear per video.

## Voice emotion with Kokoro
Kokoro has **no emotion setting**, so the emotion has to be built in. `make_audio.py` does all of this per line:

| Tool | What it does | Used on |
|---|---|---|
| **Speed** | 0.85 = dread or menace, 1.0 = normal, 1.25-1.35 = panic or excitement | Every line has its own |
| **Pitch** | +1 to +2 semitones = panic or squealing; -3 = big, menacing | L06, L08, L09, L21, L29, L31; Thief B and the hooded figure |
| **Punctuation** | "?!" rises, "..." trails off, short sentences add punch | Written into the text |
| **Stress marks** | `[not](+2)`, `[me](+2)`, `[fridge](+2)` hit the key word | 10 lines |
| **Effects** | "shout": compressed and pushed; "away": thinner with a slight echo; "echo": her "JESS!" carrying across the city; "close": intimate voice-over | Per line |
| **Level** | Shouts louder, asides quieter | Per line |
| **Human sounds** (in the sound-effects track) | Gasps, grunts and heavy breathing in the struggle, Jess's squeal, her out-of-breath panting. Kokoro can't make these, but they sell the emotion | Scene 2 especially |

**Kokoro text rules (I checked these against Kokoro's British text reader):**
- **No CAPITALS.** "Shut UP" is read as "Shut U-P". Use lowercase, and the `[word](+2)` stress mark instead.
- **Numbers as words.** "Biscuit123" is read "Biscuit one twenty-three", so the script sends "biscuit one two three".
- **"Hong Kong" needs its pronunciation spelled out** as `[Hong Kong](/hˌɒŋ kˈɒŋ/)`. It's already in the file.

**If the five big lines still sound flat** (L06, L08, L09, L21, L31): Kokoro can't truly scream. The free fix is **Chatterbox**, an open-source voice generator with an emotion "exaggeration" slider that can copy a voice from a short sample:
1. Render a 10-second Kokoro clip of `bf_emma`.
2. Give it to Chatterbox as the voice sample.
3. Generate those five lines with exaggeration around 0.7.

The voice stays the same character, just with real panic. Alternatively, record the lines yourself on your phone and convert them to her voice with a free voice changer (RVC).

**Auditions:** `python make_audio.py --alts` also renders alternative wordings for L01 and L06 into `out/alts/`, so you can pick the best take.

## Lip sync in Blender
1. `make_audio.py` writes **one voice file per character**, already lined up on the video's timeline (`out/stems/voice_YOU.wav` and so on), plus each character's dialogue text.
2. Install the **Rhubarb Lip Sync** Blender add-on. For each character: pick their face object, load their voice file and dialogue text, and bake. It keys mouth shapes onto the timeline.
3. **Check the Rocketbox head for shape keys** (Object Data → Shape Keys). If it has mouth or viseme shapes, map Rhubarb's 9 mouth shapes onto them. If not, map them to **jaw-bone rotation** (open/closed amounts). That's enough because lips are ON only in short, 1-3 s shots.
4. Only animate the lines marked **ON**. Everything else is off-camera or voice-over.
5. On L01, the mouth keeps moving during the bleep. That's deliberate: viewers lip-read the swear.

## Sound rules (voices always on top)
| Layer | Level | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| **Voices** | Every line normalised: normal -16 LUFS, shouts -14.5, asides -18.5 | Always the loudest thing |
| **Sound effects** | Peaks at least **3 dB below** the voice peaks; overall about **10 dB under** the voice | Big moments (the bus, the hit) get their punch in the gaps between lines, never on top of a word |
| **Music** | **14 dB under the voice while someone talks, 8 dB under in the gaps.** A dip around 2 kHz leaves space for words | `make_audio.py` ducks it automatically |
| **Final** | -14 LUFS integrated, peaks below -1 dBTP | TikTok's normal loudness |

No dead space:
- No silence longer than 0.3 s.
- People overlap in the struggle (L04, L07, L11, L21).
- Every scene change has voice or music running across it.

## Directing: the snatch (updated for the huge Thief B)
**Foreshadowing (viewers see it, she doesn't):**
- **0:00:** Thief A sits still on the yellow e-bike at the kerb behind her, watching.
- **On "Now you judge this":** the focus shifts off her for half a second. Thief B, towering over the bus-stop crowd, pockets his phone and nods to A.
- **During L03:** B walks towards her through the crowd, head and shoulders above everyone.

**The struggle:**
1. **Bump (L04):** she walks into B like a wall and bounces back half a step. His tiny sweet "Ooh, sorry, love!" pulls her attention up and away.
2. **Grab (L06-L07):** A rolls in on the bike and grabs. She clamps on with both hands: a 1.5 s tug-of-war, the bike wobbling, gasps from the crowd.
3. **Free (L08):** the phone spins through the air in slow motion for 6 frames, and B catches it one-handed, way above her reach.
4. **Wipe:** a red double-decker bus passes in front of the camera.
5. **Split (L09):** A bikes right and B runs left, both clutching something to their chest. Whip pans follow her head.
6. **Bystanders (L10-L11):** they point in opposite directions, talking over each other.
7. **Stop (L12-L13):** she gives up, hands on knees. "Size of a fridge" while the camera shows B's huge back disappearing into the crowd.
8. **Camera leaves her (L14):** it chases B, and she shrinks in the background, shouting.

The theft is shown as an outcome only, with no technical detail, and both thieves take part equally.

The rest of the directing (journey, sofa, rewatch detail, cliffhanger) is the same as draft 2. The one change: there's no full-screen "3 months later" card. A small corner tag appears while she talks.

## How to make the audio
```
cd drafts/phoneverse-found-text/audio
pip install kokoro soundfile scipy numpy      # plus ffmpeg on PATH
python music_garage.py                         # music beds + stings -> out/
python make_audio.py                           # every line, stems, timeline, captions, rough mix -> out/
python make_audio.py --only L06 L21            # redo a couple of lines after tweaking them
```
Outputs:
- `out/rough_mix.wav`: listen to the whole video's sound before animating anything.
- `out/timeline.json`: start and end time of every line, to block the shots.
- `out/stems/`: per-character voices for Rhubarb.
- `out/words.json`: word timings for one-word-at-a-time captions.
- `out/captions.srt`
