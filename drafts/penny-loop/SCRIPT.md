# The Penny Loop (sample script, draft 1)

**Premise:** A tourist drops a penny off the Empire State Building for a cheerful "what if" explainer, but the penny stops in mid-air, and someone down on the frozen street says he has done this 41 times already.

**Format:** a fake explainer (0:00-0:15), then the pattern interrupt, then a dialogue-driven mystery, ending in a loop and a cliffhanger. Target **62-64 s**, vertical 1080x1920, 30 fps.

## Questionnaire (answers picked for this sample)
| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Premise in one line | "A penny dropped off the Empire State Building stops in mid-air, and only one person on the street can still move." Friend test: "wait, then what?" Yes. |
| Main character | **Dale**, a tourist (green windbreaker, foam Statue of Liberty crown). Plus **the girl in the yellow raincoat** (no name, that's the mystery) and **the Narrator** (heard, never seen). |
| Place | Empire State Building, 86th-floor open-air deck, sunny afternoon, crowded with tourists. Second set: the busy street 86 floors below. |
| Hottest angle | "Could a penny from the Empire State Building kill you?" A well-known what-if that already has animated explainers, so viewers think they know where it's going. That's what makes the swerve hit. |
| Moving number | `1,050 FT` → `MPH 0→25` (freezes at 25) → `LOOP 41` → `LOOP 42` |
| Ending (comments) | Who is she? Who is the narrator? Why is she next to him now? Why is she soaking wet on a sunny day? |
| Series | Each Part opens with the same narrator line and a higher loop number. Part 2 = "LOOP 43". |

## Voices
- **Narrator:** bright, fast documentary voice (3.8 words/s), turning nervous, then sweetly creepy. The only voice in the first 15 s.
- **Dale:** ordinary, a bit nasal, gets more panicked.
- **Girl:** calm and tired, like someone who has lived this 41 times. Shouts once.

`*word*` = the one word per line that turns yellow in the captions.

**Word count:** 159 spoken words in about 62 s (2.6 words/s; the reference viral short ran about 2.4). The narrator runs at 3.8 words/s. The silences and reaction shots slow the average down on purpose, and the energy comes from cutting every 2-3 s.

## Facts and sources
- A penny's top speed is about 25 mph because it tumbles; physicist Louis Bloomfield, reported in *Scientific American* ("Could a Penny Dropped Off a Skyscraper Actually Kill You?").
- 86th-floor deck height of about 1,050 ft: widely cited; check against the building's official site before rendering.
- "The wind can blow coins back onto the deck": commonly attributed to Bloomfield too. **Verify before rendering**, or reword as "the wind up here is so strong...".
- Story, characters and every line are our own. Only the structure (fake explainer → swerve → cliffhanger) is borrowed.
