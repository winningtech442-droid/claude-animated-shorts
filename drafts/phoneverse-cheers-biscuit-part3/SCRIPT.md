# Phoneverse: "Cheers, Biscuit" Part 3, "Free Upgrade" (draft 1)

**Premise:** Jess gets a call: "free upgrade, just read me the code"… and she's already reading it out.

**Real safety point:** your network will never ask you to read out a code. In this scam the caller offers a free upgrade, sends a code, and asks you to read it back. That code actually lets them move your number to their SIM (a "SIM swap"). Your phone drops to **No Service**, and every security code your bank texts you goes to them. The fix, straight away:
1. Ring your network from another phone, using the number on their official website or app, not one from a text.
2. Then ring your bank.

| Ending | A "bank" rings. Jess hangs up to ring back on the number on her card. You: "What if that was really them?" Jess: "Then they won't mind, will they?" Hard cut |
| Series | "PART 3" tag; a cyclist makes You flinch (Part 1); "Hiya, is that Jess?" (Part 2's threat); "Oi, camera" for the third time; no spoken Part 4 tease |

**How it differs from Parts 1-2:** no link, no password, no Apple. The target is Jess, not You. It's a phone call, a code and losing your number. The shape is different too: not a race against a click, but watching a mistake happen in slow motion, then fixing it. The camera dive goes through her earpiece instead of a screen.

**Length:** 184 spoken words. At Part 2's pace that's about 58 s of speech. With about 14 s of action (the dive, the code, No Service, the ring), it comes to **about 72 s**, over a minute, so it qualifies for Creator Rewards.

**Dramatised (flag):**
- In real life, getting your number back can take longer than a minute. The episode squeezes the network blocking the swap, his SIM dying and her signal returning into a few seconds. The post caption gives the real steps.
- The mute gag and the "that's for strangers" line are drama. The "never share this code" warning in the text is typical of real code texts, but not any one company's exact wording.

**Post caption (draft):** "Your network will never ask you to read out a code. That 'upgrade' code can move your number to a criminal's SIM, and your bank's security texts go with it. Phone suddenly says No Service? Ring your network from another phone (number from their official website or app), then your bank. Anyone rushing you on the phone: hang up and ring back on a number you trust, or dial 159 for your bank. Forward scam texts to 7726. You, Jess and the scammer are fictional; the scam is real."
