# EP22 Typed Money: facts (checked 28 Nov 2026)

Every fact the episode states, where it appears, and its source. Re-check anything older than 2 days on post day.

1. **Banks don't simply lend out savers' money; a new loan creates new money.**
   - Bank of England Quarterly Bulletin 2014 Q1, "Money creation in the modern economy" (McLeay, Radia, Thomas):
     "Whenever a bank makes a loan, it simultaneously creates a matching deposit in the borrower's bank account,
     thereby creating new money." And: "banks do not act simply as intermediaries, lending out deposits that savers
     place with them" ([BoE article](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/quarterly-bulletin/2014/q1/money-creation-in-the-modern-economy)).
   - Bank of England explainer "How is money created?" ([BoE explainer](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/explainers/how-is-money-created)).
   - Where: the opener's myth ("you might think it lends your money to someone else"), the Manager's twist line
     ("...that we don't lend out *your* money") and "I *typed* it" (on screen: a balance going from £0 to £20,000 while
     the jar never moves). "Typed" is the simplification: the bank credits the borrower's account, and that new deposit
     is new money.
2. **Notes and coins are only about 3 % of the UK's money; the rest is money in bank accounts.**
   - BoE explainer above: notes and coins are about 3 % of the money in the UK economy. The 2014 article puts bank
     deposits at about 97 % of broad money.
   - Cross-check for today: Bank of England notes in circulation were about £100.5 bn on 11 Nov 2026
     ([BoE Weekly Report, 11 Nov 2026](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/weekly-report/2026/11-november-2026)). I could not
     pull the latest broad money (M4) total, so today's exact share is not confirmed; the script says "three quid in a
     hundred", the Bank's own rounded figure. Re-check before voicing if the October Money and Credit release is out.
   - Where: the vault board "CASH: £3 IN EVERY £100" and the Manager's line in the vault (0:33).
3. **Paying a loan back makes that money disappear.**
   - BoE 2014 article: "Just as taking out a new loan creates money, the repayment of bank loans destroys money."
   - Where: the Manager in the twist ("And when he pays it back, it *vanishes*").
4. **(Description only, not voiced) There are limits on how much banks can create, and savings are protected.**
   - Limits: banks must stay profitable, are bound by regulation, and the Bank of England's interest rate is "the
     ultimate limit on money creation" (BoE 2014 article).
   - FSCS protects up to £120,000 per person, per authorised bank, for banks failing on or after 1 Dec 2025
     ([Bank of England / PRA, Nov 2025](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/news/2025/november/pra-confirms-fscs-deposit-limit-to-be-increased-to-120000-from-1-december)).
   - Why it's in the description: the what-if world has a run on the bank. The real Britain has protection, so the post
     says so to avoid scaring anyone.

Not stated as fact: the what-if world (banks can only lend the cash in their vault, savers' jars handed to borrowers,
the run on the bank) is the myth made physical. It is framed as hypothetical, and the twist tells us the real Britain
works differently.
