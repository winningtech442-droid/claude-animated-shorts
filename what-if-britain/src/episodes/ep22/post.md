## Description (copy and paste)

Where does your money go when you pay it into the bank? A revolving door spins Alfie into a Britain where banks can
only lend the cash in their vault: his coin jar becomes a builder's van loan, the vault is nearly empty, and the high
street queues in the rain for money that isn't there. Then the manager admits the secret: banks don't lend out your
savings. A new loan is brand-new money typed into the borrower's account, and paying it back makes it disappear.
That's the Bank of England's own explanation. Only about £3 in every £100 is notes and coins; the rest is money in
accounts. Banks can't create money without limit (profits, rules and interest rates hold them back), and savings are
protected up to £120,000 per person, per bank, by the FSCS. Sources: Bank of England. An animated what-if, not
financial advice. Made with AI.
