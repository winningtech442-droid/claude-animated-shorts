# EP21 Mattress Money: facts (checked 27 Nov 2026)

Every fact the episode states, where it appears, and its source. Re-check anything older than 2 days on post day.

1. **Cash kept since 2016 has lost more than a quarter of its buying power.**
   - £1,000 in 2016 is worth about £1,414 today using ONS composite price data
     ([officialdata.org](https://www.officialdata.org/uk/inflation/2016)), or about £1,366 in 2025 prices using CPI
     ([inflationtool.com](https://www.inflationtool.com/british-pound/2016-to-present-value)).
   - So £1,000 kept as cash buys what £707 to £732 bought in 2016: a loss of 27-29 %. The script says
     "more than a quarter", which holds for both.
   - Before voicing: confirm with the [Bank of England inflation calculator](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/monetary-policy/inflation/inflation-calculator) (CPI).
2. **£10 of 2016 shopping costs about £14 now** (same sources: £13.66 to £14.14). Shown on the till receipt.
3. **4 in 5 Black Friday deals were the same price or cheaper at another time of the year.** (Fact card, 0:38.)
   - Which?, published 25 Nov 2026: tracked 250,000 prices for more than 750 products through last year's sales;
     "just one in five discounts were cheaper than at any other point in the year"
     ([Sky News report](https://news.sky.com/story/four-in-five-black-friday-deals-are-duds-research-finds-13615902)).
   - Corrected 27 Nov 2026: the first draft's "94 %" came from the Nov 2025 Which? study, where 94 % was the figure
     for one retailer (John Lewis), not for all deals. Do not use it as an overall figure.
4. **Prices rose 3.6 % in the 12 months to October 2026 (CPI).** Reported from the ONS release
   ([ONS release page](https://www.ons.gov.uk/releases/consumerpriceinflationukoctober2026)). Optional label on Nan's jar.

Not stated as fact: Nan's "it's been earning faster than the prices" is the principle (money keeps its buying power
only if it grows at least as fast as prices); no rate, account or product is named.
