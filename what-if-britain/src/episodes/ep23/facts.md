# EP23 Pay Later (global edition): facts (checked 28 Nov 2026)

Every fact the episode states, where it appears, and its source. Re-check anything older than 2 days on post day.
Topic family: borrowing / shopping credit (EP21 was inflation, EP22 was money creation). Global edition: prices in
dollars, no country-specific rules voiced, no provider named.

1. **"Pay in 4" is interest-free for the shopper, and the shop pays the pay-later firm a fee of about 2-8 % of the
   sale (card fees are about 1.5-3.5 %).** The pay-later firm pays the shop up front and in full, minus that fee.
   - Marketplace (3 Dec 2025), quoting eMarketer analyst Grace Broadbent: pay-later fees "range between 2-8% of the
     sales cost", against card processing fees "from 1.5-3.5%". The same story says retailers are paid up front and
     the provider carries the risk of non-payment
     ([Marketplace](https://www.marketplace.org/story/2025/12/03/how-are-retailers-benefitting-from-the-buy-now-pay-later-boom)).
   - Cross-checks: NPR (15 Dec 2022): stores pay about 2 % to card companies but "up to 8%" to pay-later providers
     ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2022/12/15/1142469148/buy-now-pay-later-hottest-holiday-shopping-trend));
     Checkout.com: "typically in the region of 2-8% of the sale cost"
     ([Checkout.com](https://www.checkout.com/resources/blog/how-does-buy-now-pay-later-bnpl-work-for-merchants)).
   - CFPB (Sept 2022) on pay-in-four: typically four interest-free instalments
     ([CFPB presentation](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_buy-now-pay-later-market-trends-and-consumer-impacts_presentation_2022-11.pdf)).
   - Where: the opener's myth ("you might think nobody's making money, because there's no interest"); the Owner's
     twist line ("...that the *shop* pays them"); the receipt "JACKET $100 / FEE -$8 / SHOP $92" (the top of the
     range, so the Owner says "*up to* eight"); "Up to eight, every hundred".

2. **Miss a payment and you can be charged a late fee.**
   - CFPB (15 Sept 2022): most pay-later loans "are subject to late fees if a borrower misses a payment"
     ([CFPB press release](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/about-us/newsroom/cfpb-study-details-the-rapid-growth-of-buy-now-pay-later-lending/)).
     CFPB's Dec 2025 data spotlight says late fees became less frequent in 2023 as use grew
     ([CFPB, Dec 2025](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_bnpl-market-report_2025-12.pdf)).
   - Not every provider charges one, and the amounts differ by country, so nothing voiced gives an amount.
   - Where: the Rep's "And you missed *one*" and the red LATE FEE sticker (2 s), Mate's "A *late* fee?"

3. **People spend more when pay-later is on offer. That is why shops pay the higher fee.**
   - Di Maggio, Williams and Katz (Harvard Business School), NBER Working Paper 30508 (2022): pay-later use "causes a
     permanent increase in total spending of around $60 per week", mostly on retail
     ([NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w30508); quote via [Payments Dive](https://www.paymentsdive.com/news/bnpl-payments-risks-trends-harvard-study-buy-now-pay-later/637686/)).
   - CFPB (2022): pay-later "is engineered to encourage consumers to purchase more and borrow more"
     ([CFPB press release](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/about-us/newsroom/cfpb-study-details-the-rapid-growth-of-buy-now-pay-later-lending/)).
   - Marketplace (above): retailers accept the fee because it improves checkout conversion and they sell more to each customer.
   - Where: the Owner's "Because you buy *more*" and the cut to Mate's overloaded trolley.

4. **"The never-never" is old British slang for buying on instalments (hire purchase).**
   - Collins English Dictionary via Dictionary.com: never-never, British, "the hire-purchase system of buying"
     ([Dictionary.com](https://www.dictionary.com/browse/never-never)). Green's Dictionary of Slang gives the joke
     behind the name: one never finishes paying ([Green's](https://greensdictofslang.com/entry/tica3wq)).
   - Where: Nan at the car boot sale, and the Rep's cliffhanger line. Nan's "You *never* finish paying" is the joke in
     the nickname, not a claim about pay-later today.

5. **(Description only, not voiced) This season's scale.** Adobe forecasts a record $21.3 bn of US online pay-later
   spending for 1 Nov to 31 Dec 2026, about 8 % of holiday spending
   ([Adobe holiday report](https://business.adobe.com/resources/holiday-shopping-report.html);
   [Adobe forecast PDF](https://business.adobe.com/assets/resources/holiday-shopping-report/2026/pdf/2026-adobe-digital-insights-holiday-forecast.pdf)).
   Cyber Monday 2025 set the single-day record at $1.03 bn (Adobe, via
   [ConsumerAffairs](https://www.consumeraffairs.com/news/holiday-shoppers-embraced-planning-over-impulse-spending-as-bnpl-use-surged-120225.html)).
   Re-check with Adobe's actual Cyber Week numbers on post day.

Not stated as fact: the what-if world (every till takes pay-later only, letterboxes full of instalment letters, a
Rep who scans pins) is the myth made physical. It is framed as hypothetical, and the twist shows the real world: the
shop pays the fee because you spend more.
