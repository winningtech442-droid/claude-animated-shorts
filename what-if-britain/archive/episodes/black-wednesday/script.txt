This week, the Bank of England decided your mortgage *rate*.
When it moves, it's usually a quarter of a *percent*.
Now imagine it heading for *fifteen*. In one *day*.
That really happened. Wednesday, the sixteenth of September, *1992*.
Britain had tied the pound to Germany's *mark*.
The promise: keep the pound strong, *whatever* it costs.
But traders didn't believe it. They started *selling* pounds.
Eleven a.m. Rates jump from ten to twelve *percent*.
The pound keeps *falling*.
So the government promises *fifteen*.
Still *falling*.
The Bank spends billions buying pounds to hold it *up*.
Seven forty p.m. Britain gives up. We're *out*.
The day cost us three point three billion *pounds*.
And traders who bet against us made a *fortune*.
So what if we'd held *on*?
To keep the pound in, rates stay *sky-high*.
A two-hundred-thousand-pound mortgage at fifteen percent? Two thousand five hundred and sixty pounds a *month*.
At around today's rates? About twelve hundred and *thirty*.
That's thirteen hundred pounds more. Every single *month*.
More homes *lost*. More firms shut *down*.
But that's not what happened.
The next day, rates fell back to *ten*.
Borrowing got *cheaper*, and Britain started to *grow*.
Some even call it White *Wednesday*.
The worst day for the pound...
might have been the best day for your *wallet*.
