DESCRIPTION (paste as the caption)
America lost money on Britain's war loan. Yes, really. After the Second World War Britain was skint, and asked America for a gift. America said no, and lent four point three billion dollars at two per cent instead, paid back one instalment a year. Britain finally finished paying in 2006, and in total sent back about seven and a half billion dollars, nearly double what it borrowed. Sounds like a win for America. But while Britain paid, American prices rose roughly tenfold, so every dollar sent back bought less than the one that was lent. Count the repayments in the dollars of the day the loan was made and, by our own rough sum, they come to about two and a half billion: little more than half. Britain got a cheap loan. America got paid in shrinking dollars. So who won? Britain or America? Send this to an American and ask them. Made with AI.

PINNED COMMENT
Sources and our own sums. The 1945 loan: $3.75bn credit + $650m Lend-Lease settlement = $4.33bn at 2%, repaid yearly from 1951, six payments deferred, last one 29 Dec 2006 (UK gov History blog; Anglo-American loan records). Total repaid about $7.5bn (BBC figures, 2006), about 1.7x the loan, so "nearly double" is generous. US prices: CPI 19.5 (1946) to 201.6 (2006), about x10. "Little more than half" is OUR rough sum: each year's instalment converted back into 1946 dollars by US CPI comes to about $2.4-2.6bn, versus $4.33bn lent. In plain dollars America got more back than it lent. It "lost" only in real, after-inflation money. History, not advice.
