# FIX #15 post caption (TikTok, paste as is)

One code, and your card can end up on a stranger's phone. This is digital wallet fraud, and it is hitting UK phones this Black Friday. It starts with a text or an ad for a deal that looks too good: 70% off, ends tonight. You tap the link and type in your card details, and at that same moment a scammer types them into Apple Pay or Google Wallet on their own phone. Your bank isn't sure it's really you, so it texts you a one-time passcode. The fake page asks you to enter it, or a fake "fraud team" calls and asks you to read it out. Once they have that code, your card works on their phone and they can tap to pay in shops and online, while the card in your pocket still works too. Which? reported one man who lost more than £18,000 in a few hours this way. How to stay safe: read the whole text before you use any code. If it mentions adding your card to a digital wallet and that isn't you, stop. No real bank will ever ask you to read out or type in a code for them. If you get an alert that your card was added to a wallet on a new device, call the number on the back of your card straight away; your bank can switch off the card on their phone. Forward scam texts free to 7726 and report fraud to Report Fraud at reportfraud.police.uk, or call 101 in Scotland.
